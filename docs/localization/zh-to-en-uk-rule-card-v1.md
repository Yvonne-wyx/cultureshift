# Chinese-to-English (UK) localization rule card v1

**Scope:** static ads for AI software/apps localized from China for a UK context. This is an operational drafting aid, not legal advice or evidence of cultural acceptance.

## Non-negotiable boundary

Brand Lock remains unchanged: **logo**, **product name**, **verified product facts**, **real UI**, **benefit order**, **CTA action meaning**, and **layout template**. Only narrative, use scenario, verified trust information, and language may be proposed for localization. Human review is required before use.

## Source-backed rules

These rules follow directly from the cited source. They are not claims about what all UK audiences prefer.

| ID | Rule for the English draft | Basis and limit | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ZEU-S1 | Use clear, plain English: prefer familiar words, explain unavoidable technical terms, keep chunks short, and split sentences that exceed roughly 25 words. | GOV.UK clear-language guidance supports accessibility and comprehension. It is public-sector content guidance, not ad-effectiveness evidence. [S1] | Plain-language editorial pass; preserve claim strength and Brand Lock. |
| ZEU-S2 | Use the audience's own vocabulary when research supports it; do not mechanically transliterate Chinese product-category or workflow terms. | GOV.UK recommends words users know and use. Actual ad vocabulary still requires audience/keyword research. [S1] | Record the research source or mark the word choice as a hypothesis. |
| ZEU-S3 | Keep every objective product claim no stronger than the evidence held before publication; do not omit material qualifications or rely on translation to soften/expand a claim. | CAP Code Section 3 requires substantiation for objective claims and prohibits materially misleading presentation. [S2] | Link each objective claim to verified product evidence; escalate gaps. |
| ZEU-S4 | Assess the whole ad—copy and imagery—for misleading overall impression, including representations of product UI or capability. | ASA/CAP guidance states that both specific claims and the overall impression, including imagery, can mislead. [S2] | Compare the draft with real UI and verified capability evidence. |

## Hypotheses requiring validation

Every item below is a `CulturalHypothesis`, not a rule or fact. Do not apply it by default.

| ID | CulturalHypothesis | Why uncertain | Required validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ZEU-H1 | A concrete task-led headline may be clearer than retaining an abstract or aspirational source headline. | Clarity guidance does not prove ad preference or performance for a particular UK segment. | Comprehension interviews with the defined target segment; compare both variants without changing benefit order. |
| ZEU-H2 | Direct second-person wording may feel clearer than organization-centred wording for a self-serve AI app. | Tone depends on brand, channel, role, and buying context. | Brand review plus target-user qualitative test. |
| ZEU-H3 | Specific evidence near an AI capability claim may increase perceived trust. | Trust cues vary by product maturity and audience; added evidence may overload a static layout. | Verify the evidence, then test comprehension and perceived credibility with target users. |

Record each adopted hypothesis with target segment, evidence references, uncertainty, alternatives/counterevidence, validation requirement, and `pending` review status. Generation cannot mark it approved.

## Release gate

Fail closed if product evidence, rights, provenance, target segment, Brand Lock, or required human validation is missing or conflicted. Do not claim cultural correctness, legal compliance, or performance uplift.
